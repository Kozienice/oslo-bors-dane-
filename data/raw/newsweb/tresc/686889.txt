Luxembourg  - 19 November  2026 - Subsea  7 S.A. (Oslo  Børs: SUBC,  ADR: SUBCY,
ISIN:  LU0075646355, the Company) announced today  results of Subsea7 Group (the
Group, Subsea7) for the third quarter of 2026 which ended 30 September 2026.

Highlights

  * Adjusted EBITDA of $549 million in the third quarter, an increase of 35%
    compared with the prior year period, equating to a margin of 26.5%, up from
    22.1% in the third quarter last year
  * Strong performance in Subsea and Conventional, with revenue growth of 13%
    and an Adjusted EBITDA margin of 29%, up from 24% in the third quarter
    2025. A solid performance in Renewables with revenue growth of 11% and an
    Adjusted EBITDA margin steady at 16%
  * High-quality backlog of $12.5 billion including $6.1 billion for execution
    in 2027, providing high revenue visibility
  * Strong cash conversion of 1.3x, resulting in net cash including lease
    liabilities of $734 million, up from $190 million in the second quarter 2026
  * Guidance for full year 2026 revenue raised to approximately $8 billion (from
    $7.4 to 7.8 billion) with Adjusted EBITDA margin maintained at approximately
    24%
  * As of yesterday, approvals have been received from 13 of the 16 antitrust
    authorities whose clearance is required, including most recently in Brazil
    and Egypt with the proposed merger with Saipem now expected to complete in
    early 2027
  * Sale of heavy transportation business agreed, supporting the distribution of
    a special dividend of EUR105 million, as outlined in the Common Merger Plan

Stuart Fitzgerald, Chief Executive Officer, said:

Subsea7  achieved particularly  strong results  in the  third quarter  of 2026,
delivering  revenue growth of  13% alongside margin expansion  to 26.5%, up from
22.1% a  year ago and 24.4% in the  second quarter. This strong outcome reflects
the  success  of  our  long-term  strategy,  discipline  in  tendering, focus on
optimising execution and the dedication and commitment of our people. While I am
pleased  with  the  progress  we  have  made  this  year,  we  remain focused on
delivering   further   improvements   in  both  our  operational  and  financial
performance  that will  continue to  strengthen our  track record of predictable
delivery and value creation for clients and shareholders.

Merger update
On 4 November, Subsea7 and Saipem received approval for the proposed merger from
the  Brazilian competition authority. The approval was conditional upon the sale
of  Seven Borealis and we have begun the process of identifying a buyer. As part
of  the divestment, a sale and leaseback  arrangement will be in place to ensure
Subsea7  can  continue  to  execute  its  backlog  of  existing projects without
interruption.  These commitments  currently extend  into 2028. As  of yesterday,
approvals  have  been  received  from  13 of  the 16 antitrust authorities whose
clearance  is required  with the  processes with  anti-trust authorities in West
Africa  (Economic  Community  of  West  African  States), Australia and European
Commission  continuing. The proposed merger is now expected to complete in early
2027.

Third quarter project review
In Subsea and Conventional, activity was high throughout the third quarter, with
a  vessel utilisation  of 84%. Seven  Vega was  active in  the Gulf  of America,
installing  production risers at the Monument  field. With a pipe wall thickness
of  approximately  40 millimetres,  this  project  reaffirmed  the use of reeled
pipelay  for ultra-high  pressure ('20K')  field developments.  Remaining in the
region,  Seven Vega went on  to lay pipe at  Buckskin South and began mobilising
for  Trion in Mexico.  Seven Seas also  worked on Monument  before moving to the
Ginger  field in Trinidad and Tobago. Further south, in Brazil, Seven Oceans and
Seven  Merlin made good progress at Mero  4 and Búzios 8 and 9. In Norway, Seven
Oceanic  installed various umbilicals and  equipment at Yggdrasil, Gjøa, Fenris,
Northern  Lights  and  Fram  Sør,  while  Seven  Pacific  was  active installing
flexibles,  umbilicals, and  jumpers at  Sakarya and  Mina West,  in Türkiye and
Egypt,  respectively. Seven Arctic underwent a 5-year dry dock before mobilising
to Australia and Seven Navica also underwent planned maintenance.

In  Renewables,  offshore  activity  continued  at  a  very high pace, achieving
utilisation  of 92%. Seaway Ventus  continued installation of  monopiles at East
Anglia  THREE in the UK before  undergoing planned maintenance. Seaway Strashnov
continued  monopile installation  in France  before mobilising  for Baltica 2 in
Poland.  Seaway Aimery  installed cables  at East  Anglia THREE before moving to
Baltyk  II & III in Poland. Seaway Alfa Lift installed transition pieces and pin
piles at Inch Cape in the UK.

Third quarter financial review
Revenue  was  $2.1  billion,  growth  of  13% when  compared with the prior year
period.  Adjusted EBITDA was $549 million equating to a margin of 26.5%, up from
22.1% in Q3 2025. After net losses within other gains and losses of $63 million,
primarily  driven by  non-cash losses  on embedded  derivatives, and taxation of
$101 million, net income was $223 million.

Net  cash generated from operating activities was $629 million, including a $191
million  favourable movement in net working  capital. Net cash used in investing
activities  was  $56  million,  of  which  $73  million  related to purchases of
property,  plant and equipment.  Net cash used  in financing activities was $113
million  including lease payments  of $51 million.  During the quarter, cash and
cash  equivalents  increased  by  $460  million  to  $1,506  million and, at 30
September  2026, net cash was  $734 million including  lease liabilities of $325
million.

Third  quarter order intake was approximately $900 million comprising new awards
of  approximately  $400  million  and  escalations of approximately $500 million
resulting  in a book-to-bill ratio of 0.5 times. Backlog at the end of September
was  $12.5 billion,  of which  $2.1 billion  is expected  to be  executed in the
remainder of 2026, $6.1 billion in 2027 and $4.3 billion in 2028 and beyond.

Outlook

After  a decade of  engineering optimisation and  standardisation by Subsea7 and
the  broader industry, deepwater  developments occupy a  competitive position on
the  resource cost curve, with oil price  breakevens of around $40 per barrel or
less.  Supported by  attractive economics  and relatively  low carbon intensity,
driven  in  part  by  economies  of  scale,  greenfield and brownfield deepwater
developments  are expected to remain an essential  part of the global energy mix
across  a  wide  range  of  macroeconomic  scenarios for the foreseeable future.
Recent  concerns regarding  energy security  and supply  diversification further
reinforce this view.

While  the  geopolitical  environment  remains  uncertain  and  commodity  price
volatility  is elevated, activity levels  and bidding prospects across Subsea7's
key  subsea  markets  remain  resilient.  We  therefore  expect  demand  for our
solutions  to remain  robust in  the years  ahead. Capital  discipline among our
clients,  as  well  as  by  Subsea7  and  its  peers, supports the prospect of a
prolonged  upcycle, without the  extreme highs and  lows experienced in previous
cycles.

In  this environment, Subsea7's focus on quality order intake and strong project
execution  should  continue  to  support  further  improvement  in our return on
capital  employed. Leveraging the skills  of our 2,800 experienced engineers and
project  managers, together  with a  modern, capable  fleet of vessels and their
crews,  Subsea7 remains  focused on  sustaining returns  at a level that creates
value and opportunity for its shareholders, clients and workforce.

Guidance

In  full  year  2026, we  anticipate  revenue  to  be  approximately  $8 billion
(formerly  $7.4  to  7.8 billion)  with  Adjusted  EBITDA  margin  maintained at
approximately  24%. In full year 2027, we expect  revenue to be approximately $8
billion, with an Adjusted EBITDA margin of approximately 25%.
