Record-high harvest volume and continued strong operational and biological
performance contributed to a significant improvement in results compared with
the same period last year. Higher volumes, lower cost levels and improved price
achievement contributed positively, while Sales & Industry delivered strong
results. Strong cash flow from operations also contributed to a further
reduction in net interest-bearing debt and a strengthened financial position.

Operational EBIT for the Group was NOK 1,609 million in the third quarter of
2026. Harvest volume reached a record-high 105,100 tonnes, and Operational EBIT
per kg was NOK 15.3. In Norway, Operational EBIT was NOK 1,620 million, harvest
volume was 99,900 tonnes and Operational EBIT per kg was NOK 16.2.

  * Record-high harvest volume, driven by strong biological performance
    throughout 2026.
  * Strong results from Sales & Industry, driven by record-high volumes through
    the processing facilities, improved operational performance and solid sales
    results.
  * Icelandic Salmon returned to positive results, driven by a lower cost level
    and good price achievement.
  * Significantly higher harvest volume from Scottish Sea Farms, although
    results remained weak due to biological challenges.

"Record-high harvest volume and a significant improvement in results reflect the
strong biological development throughout 2026. The underlying biological status
remains good, and together with lower costs for the fish currently in the sea,
this provides a solid foundation going forward," says Frode Arntsen, CEO of
SalMar ASA.

Strengthened financial position

SalMar further strengthened its financial position in the third quarter. Net
interest-bearing debt was reduced by NOK 1,228 million from the end of the
second quarter to NOK 17,912 million. Compared with the same period last year,
net interest-bearing debt has been reduced by NOK 3,739 million. NIBD/EBITDA was
reduced to 2.1, from 2.5 at the end of the second quarter and 4.0 at the end of
the third quarter of 2025.

At the same time, the equity ratio increased to 36.0 per cent, and SalMar had
NOK 15.9 billion in available liquidity at the end of the quarter. This provides
the company with solid financial flexibility, including for the completion of
the Måsøval transaction once the necessary regulatory approvals have been
obtained.

Expecting slightly lower cost level and guiding harvest volume of 346,000 tonnes
for 2027

For the fourth quarter of 2026, SalMar expects a slightly lower cost level in
the value chain compared with the third quarter. At the same time, lower harvest
volume in Norway is expected compared with the fourth quarter of 2025.

Harvest volume guidance for 2026 is 347,000 tonnes, including the relative share
from Scottish Sea Farms. This represents harvest volume growth of 15 per cent
compared with 2025. Guidance for Norway remains unchanged, while volume
expectations for Icelandic Salmon and Scottish Sea Farms have been somewhat
reduced.

For 2027, SalMar expects total harvest volume of 348,000 tonnes, including the
relative share from Scottish Sea Farms, in line with 2026. This includes
307,000 tonnes from Norway and SalMar Ocean, 18,000 tonnes from Icelandic Salmon
and 45,000 tonnes from Scottish Sea Farms on a 100 per cent basis. Måsøval is
not included in the harvest volume guidance for 2027, as the transaction remains
subject to relevant regulatory approvals.

SalMar expects low global supply growth in 2027 and continues to see strong
demand for its products.

Måsøval transaction process progressing according to plan

In July 2026, SalMar ASA entered into an agreement with Heimstø AS to acquire
Heimstø's 85,727,553 shares in Måsøval AS, representing approximately 70 per
cent of the share capital. The purchase price is NOK 39.50 per share,
corresponding to a total consideration of approximately NOK 3.4 billion. The
transaction is subject to customary closing conditions, including relevant
regulatory approvals. The process of obtaining the necessary regulatory
approvals is progressing according to plan.

The transaction is expected to provide SalMar with an even stronger platform for
further sustainable growth in Central Norway, with potential for improved
utilisation of resources, expertise and infrastructure, as well as further
development on the salmon's terms and long-term value creation along the
Norwegian coast.

About SalMar

SalMar is one of the world's largest and most efficient producers of salmon. The
Group has farming operations in Central Norway, Northern Norway, Offshore and
Iceland, as well as substantial harvesting and secondary processing operations.
In addition SalMar owns 50% of Scottish Sea Farms Ltd.
