Equinor (OSE: EQNR, NYSE: EQNR) updates its production guidance following
specific temporary shut-ins and operational challenges. Temporary shut-ins at
Bacalhau and Visund are expected to reduce the average production by 15-20 mboe
per day for the year. In addition, there are currently some operational
challenges at Johan Castberg.

Based on the incidents in Brazil and on the Norwegian continental shelf, Equinor
changes its production guidance back to around 3 percent growth for 2026. This
is in line with the guiding provided at the start of the year. However, it is a
reduction from the increased guiding to around 4 percent, communicated in
connection with the third quarter results following strong operational
performance across several fields.

On 18 November, a dropped-object incident occurred on the Bacalhau FPSO. No one
was injured during the incident, but equipment was affected. Equinor has
informed Brazil's industry regulator, Agência Nacional do Petróleo (ANP), that
production has been shut down due to the incident. Based on current information,
production is expected to remain shut down for approximately five to eight
weeks, depending on the actions required for a safe restart of production.
Equinor is working with its contractors to investigate the incident.

As previously communicated, the Visund field will be shut down for around two
months following a fire in the emergency power switchboard room on 4 November.

In recent days, production at Johan Castberg has been temporarily reduced to a
minimum as offloading of oil is currently not possible. Work is ongoing to
restore safe offloading.

The incidents have no impact on recoverable reserves.
